# Fireflies Outdoors Z1 Artemis TIR

## The Kind of Light Someone Who Makes Lights Carries

I make and sell flashlights.

I have a lot of flashlights. I have strong opinions about CRI, DUV, drivers, optics, clips, and whether a flashlight is actually good at being a flashlight. And when I want something specific, I usually just build it.

Before the Z1, I had been carrying a Convoy S2+ triple with Nichia 519A 3000K emitters, an 18350 tube, brass accents, and a black DLC titanium clip. It was compact, warm, high-CRI, and exactly the way I wanted it.

It was a great light.

Then I started seeing the Fireflies Outdoors Z1 Artemis everywhere.

At first glance, I thought it was a little silly.

I'm fundamentally a mechanical-switch flashlight guy. E-switches seem unnecessarily complicated. Andúril *definitely* seems unnecessarily complicated. Give me a good regulated driver, three useful modes, a forward clicky, and an emitter that doesn't make everything look like a hospital hallway, and I'm generally happy.

But I started digging into the Z1's specifications and reading reviews.

And some things caught my attention.

The Z1 is an **18650 light that's only 92 mm tall**. For perspective, a Convoy T3—a *14500* light—is about 93.3 mm tall.

That's interesting.

Then you keep going.

6063 aluminum. A proper TIR optic. A 32 W Lume X1 boost driver. Fireflies' own high-CRI emitters. RGB auxiliary LEDs. Andúril 2. All of it packaged into something barely larger than lights carrying substantially smaller cells.

Things started adding up.

So naturally, I screwed up and bought one.

Mine is equipped with the **FFL5009R 3500K Rosy, CRI95** emitter. I charged a pair of Molicel P30Bs for it and started figuring out Andúril.

And as someone who knows how to code, Andúril wasn't actually that hard to understand. It's basically a state machine controlled by one button. Once I stopped treating the flowchart like evidence of a psychological disorder, it started making sense.

Then something unfortunate happened:

**The Z1 made parts of my existing flashlight inventory stop making sense.**

## Pure EDC Joy

The Z1 is just an exceptionally enjoyable EDC light.

More importantly, underneath all the enthusiast-light weirdness, it's a *competent flashlight*.

The FFL5009R and Lume X1 combination is excellent. Regulation is good. The beam is useful. Color rendering is excellent. And the "Rosy" emitter is actually rosy rather than aggressively pink or magenta.

It makes things look good without making them look strange.

That's an important distinction.

The TIR gives the Z1 a very practical general-purpose beam. There's enough concentration to put useful light downrange, but enough spill that it remains excellent indoors and at normal walking distances. I don't find myself thinking about the optic while I'm using it.

That's probably the highest compliment I can give an optic.

It just works.

## And Then I Started Liking the Weird Stuff

I expected to tolerate Andúril.

Instead, I've ended up using features that I previously considered enthusiast-light nonsense.

Lockout, for example, is genuinely useful on an E-switch EDC light.

I've also configured **Power-Off Voltage Display**. When I switch the Z1 off, its RGB auxiliary LEDs briefly illuminate in a color corresponding to cell voltage.

So I turn the flashlight off and immediately get a rough indication of how much battery I've used.

That's actually useful.

No voltage menu. No counting blinks. No wondering whether I should swap the cell before leaving the house.

Turn it off.

**Blue. Fine.**

And yes, it has RGB auxiliary LEDs.

I know.

Mine are normally set to aqua.

Because aqua, bro.

And once configured the way I want it, I don't have to think about any of this. The complicated interface effectively disappears during normal use. Click for on. Click for off. Hold to change output. The deeper functionality is there when I want it and stays out of the way when I don't.

That's how enthusiast features should work.

## It Also Feels Like an Object

This is something specifications don't communicate very well.

The Z1 feels *finished*.

It's compact and dense without feeling excessively heavy. The machining is good. The proportions are right. Nothing about it feels like a collection of enthusiast flashlight components stuffed into a tube.

It feels like somebody designed a flashlight.

Fireflies doesn't exactly have a legendary reputation for pocket clips, but the Z1's clip is actually decent. It carries well, the light disappears surprisingly easily in a pocket for an 18650 platform, and I've never felt an immediate need to replace the clip.

Again: high praise from someone who has replaced a lot of flashlight clips.

## The Thing Fireflies Got Right

The Z1 sold me on Fireflies for one fundamental reason:

**They remembered that it has to be a good flashlight first.**

It can have Andúril.

It can have RGB auxiliary LEDs.

It can tell me battery voltage by glowing different colors.

It can have candle mode, lightning mode, beacon modes, configurable ramps, thermal configuration, electronic lockout, and enough settings to entertain someone on a long flight.

None of that matters if the actual flashlight sucks.

The Z1 doesn't.

The emitter is excellent. The driver is excellent. The optic works. The size is remarkable for an 18650 light. The beam is useful. The light carries well. The controls become intuitive once configured.

All of the enthusiast features sit *on top of* a genuinely excellent flashlight instead of compensating for one that isn't.

That's the difference.

## Where It Fits

The Z1 Artemis is now my default carry light.

Not my only light.

There are situations where I want substantially more throw, substantially more output, a mechanical switch, a particular beam profile, or something I'm willing to beat mercilessly without caring what happens to it.

That's why I own—and build—different flashlights.

But if I'm leaving the house and don't have a particular mission requirement?

I grab the Z1.

It's small enough that carrying an 18650 doesn't feel like carrying an 18650. The P30B gives it plenty of energy and current capability. The boost driver and high-CRI emitter make that energy useful. The TIR gives it a genuinely versatile beam. And Andúril, somewhat against my better judgment, gives me a few features I've discovered I actually like.

That's what ultimately makes the Z1 interesting to me.

I'm not impressed because it's a weird enthusiast flashlight.

I'm impressed because it's a **fantastic flashlight that also happens to be a weird enthusiast flashlight.**

And for someone who makes his own lights because commercial lights usually aren't quite what he wants?

That's saying something.
